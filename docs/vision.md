# LBICAA — Project Vision

## 1. Purpose

LBICAA is a claims-centred research and knowledge system designed to collect, structure, examine and preserve information about real-world entities, organisations, events and relationships.

Its central concern is not simply what information is available, but:

- What exactly is being claimed?
- Which sources support that claim?
- Is there evidence that contradicts it?
- What is documented, inferred or merely hypothesised?
- How reliable is the available evidence?
- When was a statement valid, and when did the system record it?

LBICAA aims to make research traceable, reproducible and open to scrutiny.

## 2. Core principle: claims before conclusions

Information should not become an unquestioned fact merely because it appears in a source or has been repeated by multiple parties.

LBICAA treats claims as explicit, independently assessable records.

A claim connects a subject, a predicate and an object. It also carries information about its nature, status, confidence and temporal validity.

Claims can be supported, contradicted or contextualised by evidence. Conflicting claims are retained rather than silently overwritten.

## 3. Epistemic distinctions

LBICAA distinguishes three kinds of claims:

- **Documented:** explicitly stated or recorded in an identifiable source.
- **Inferred:** derived through reasoning from available information.
- **Hypothesis:** a proposition requiring further investigation.

These distinctions must remain visible. An inference must not silently become a documented fact.

Confidence is recorded separately from claim nature. A documented statement can still have uncertain reliability, and a hypothesis can be worth investigating without being treated as established.

## 4. Transparency and accountability

The system is intended to preserve:

- Source identity and provenance.
- Evidence locations and relevant excerpts.
- Supporting and contradicting assessments.
- Verification methods and outcomes.
- Temporal validity and recording dates.
- Licensing and reuse information.

The objective is that a reader can follow a conclusion back through its claims and evidence to the underlying sources.

## 5. Scope

LBICAA is intended to support structured research, source comparison, knowledge organisation and the examination of complex relationships.

It is not intended to replace courts, regulators, professional investigators or qualified legal judgement.

It must not present automated analysis as authoritative proof.

## 6. Long-term direction

The intended direction includes:

- Persistent storage and versioned claim histories.
- Source discovery and controlled crawling.
- Evidence extraction and verification workflows.
- Cross-source comparison and analytical services.
- Interfaces for inspecting claims, evidence and relationships.
- Transparent publication of appropriately governed research data.

These are development objectives, not assertions that all such capabilities are already implemented.

## 7. Guiding rule

**Every meaningful conclusion should be traceable to the claims, evidence and sources on which it depends.**
